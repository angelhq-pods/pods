Method (agreed 9 Nov 2026, America/Denver)

Personal tool only. Not a company. Last source-review date belongs on the published Method page and must be updated when the corpus changes.

Purpose

Help the reader compare how major Christian traditions, and two named scholarly tracks, read a verse or a belief. The tool describes teachings. It does not issue a ruling, declare a winner, or give pastoral advice.

Text first (verse queries)

  • Show the verse before any interpretation column.
  • At least two translations, plus a literal or original-language line.
  • Public-domain or clearly licensed translations only. Never paste a copyrighted translation.
  • The tool helps the user read Scripture; it does not replace the text with commentary.

Queries

  1. Verse input.
  2. Free-text belief input (second use case).
  3. Belief queries must show a short notice: this describes teachings; it does not issue a ruling or give pastoral advice. Especially on contested moral questions.

Columns

  • Eastern Orthodox. Note Oriental Orthodox when they differ.
  • Catholic.
  • Protestant: one heading, but if Lutherans, Reformed, Anglicans, Baptists, and Pentecostals or Wesleyans disagree, show those rows. Never one blended Protestant answer.
  • Scholar section: split-check of two named tracks, not a blended “consensus” column.
  • Track A: historical-critical journals (named list).
  • Track B: evangelical academic journals (named list).
  • If both agree: one statement, “Both academic tracks teach X,” with citations from each.
  • If they disagree: show both, labeled, each with sources and the actual argument. Never average them. Never call either track “the consensus.”

Official teaching vs opinion

  • Catholic: distinguish magisterial teaching from theological opinion.
  • Orthodox: distinguish dogma from theologoumena.
  • Protestant: name the confession or official statement being cited.
  • “Catholics believe” is too coarse on contested questions.

Retrieve-and-cite

  • Every displayed claim is retrieved from a fixed corpus and cited to an openable source.
  • Corpus: Catechism paragraphs, councils, liturgies, confessions, and the two named journal sets (plus the Bible translations in use).
  • If a source cannot be opened, do not display the claim.
  • Do not generate unsourced paraphrases from a model’s memory.
  • Label every claim: Scripture, official church teaching, history, or interpretation.
  • For each tradition, show the verses and arguments it actually uses, not only the conclusion.

Scholar weighting

  • A work enters and is weighted in the Scholar section by academic tests only: the named journals, citations inside those academic networks, and academic honors such as the Burkitt Medal, SBL prizes, BAS scholarly book awards, or a university prize like the Grawemeyer.
  • Religious prizes (for example a Catholicism prize or a Christianity Today book award) belong only in the matching tradition column.
  • The same work may appear in both places. The religious prize does not qualify it as scholarship.

Canon and history

  • Mention canon differences only when they change the reading (books Catholics or Orthodox include and Protestants do not).
  • Optional “history of interpretation” row (early, medieval, Reformation, modern) only on famous disputed verses. Do not lead with it.

Presentation rules

  • Publish this method on the app: journal lists, the split-check rule, and the date of the last source review.
  • Never hide a column to match the user’s denomination. A highlight is fine. A filter that conceals the others is not.
  • If used by a group: a shareable or printable one-page comparison for a study night. No streaks, chat, or daily devotionals.
  • No Jewish or Second Temple column at launch. Plan it later for Old Testament queries only.

Starter journal lists (named tracks)

Historical-critical

Journal of Biblical Literature; New Testament Studies; Vetus Testamentum; Journal for the Study of the Old Testament; Journal for the Study of the New Testament; Novum Testamentum; Zeitschrift für die neutestamentliche Wissenschaft; Journal of Theological Studies; Biblica; Catholic Biblical Quarterly; Dead Sea Discoveries; Revue Biblique.

Evangelical academic

Tyndale Bulletin; Journal of the Evangelical Theological Society; Bulletin for Biblical Research; Westminster Theological Journal; Bibliotheca Sacra.

Safeguards

  • Unlisted personal app (noindex, nofollow). Do not market it.
  • No pastoral or counselor voice.
  • Quote short excerpts only, with a link to the openable source. Do not copy copyrighted books, journals, or the Catechism in full.
