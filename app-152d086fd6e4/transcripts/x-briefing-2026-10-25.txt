Good morning. This is your X briefing for Sunday, October twenty-fifth. I read a hundred and thirty-one posts from Mike Cernovich, Adam Townsend, and BowTiedBull from the last day, so you don't have to. Here's what matters.

The headline: Cernovich got his biggest reach from a pile-on against Airbnb, about eight hundred thousand views. But the post his audience actually rallied behind was about the Alaska Senate race.

Let's start with Cernovich. He posted a hundred times: six original posts, thirty-four quote posts, twenty replies, and forty reposts.

His biggest topic was Airbnb. He wrote seven posts saying it became too stressful to stay at one, that hosts turned into slumlords, and that he hopes the company fails. Two of those posts passed three hundred thousand views each. The background: on October twentieth, Airbnb announced that starting with bookings made on November twentieth, hosts can no longer require guests to wash dishes, strip beds, start laundry, or haul trash off-site. Cernovich's posts landed while that story was already trending.

His second topic was Alaska. He posted that Democrats put a man named Dan Sullivan on the ballot against the Republican senator, who is also named Dan Sullivan, and that there was zero media coverage. That post got almost nine thousand likes and more than eleven hundred reposts. Here are the facts behind it. On October twenty-second, the Justice Department sued to block certification of the Alaska Senate result if votes for the second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, could have flipped the ranked-choice outcome. In June, Alaska's own Division of Elections found the candidacy was meant to confuse voters, but the state Supreme Court kept him on the ballot. He got two and a half percent in the primary. The incumbent is labeled on the ballot as the registered Republican incumbent, and the second Sullivan has no party label. President Trump said he personally ordered the suit. On the media-coverage claim, AP, NBC, and CNBC all covered the lawsuit on October twenty-third.
